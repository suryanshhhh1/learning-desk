# Module 9: NABH Chapters in Plain Language, Part 2

This module covers the six chapters about running the hospital: IPC, PSQ, ROM, FMS, HRM and IMS. They hold 71 objective elements. Most of the documents, registers and trackers we prepare for clients come from these chapters.

### Chapter 5: Infection Prevention and Control (IPC)

| Standard | In simple words | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IPC.1 | The hospital runs an infection control programme with enough resources | Infection control committee and nurse, infection control manual, hand hygiene audits at least monthly, a wash basin or hand rub in every patient area, tracking of hospital acquired infections |
| IPC.2 | Infection control is applied in support services | Kitchen, laundry, housekeeping and CSSD procedures, biomedical waste segregated by colour at the source and handed to an authorised vendor |

Module 11 explains infection control and waste in more detail.

### Chapter 6: Patient Safety and Quality Improvement (PSQ)

| Standard | In simple words | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PSQ.1 | The hospital runs a patient safety and quality programme | Quality committee meeting every month, incident reporting system, list of sentinel events, clinical audits |
| PSQ.2 | Key indicators are measured to improve care | KPI register with a numerator, denominator and formula for each indicator, monthly data, action taken when a number gets worse |

Module 10 explains these tools.

### Chapter 7: Responsibility of Management (ROM)

| Standard | In simple words | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ROM.1 | The responsibilities of those who govern are defined | Organogram, named heads of departments, a written vision and mission |
| ROM.2 | Leaders run the hospital ethically and within the law | Valid registrations and licences, a licence tracker, a code of conduct, transparent billing and tariff |
| ROM.3 | Leaders build patient safety and quality into care | Management reviews quality data and acts on it, and a person is named in charge of quality |
| ROM.4 | Management plans for sustainability | Steps to save energy and water, reduce waste, and plan for the hospital's long-term finances and social role |

### Chapter 8: Facility Management and Safety (FMS)

| Standard | In simple words | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FMS.1 | The building is safe for patients, visitors and staff | Facility safety rounds, signage, railings, a safe and clean environment |
| FMS.2 | Engineering, support services and equipment are managed | Equipment list, maintenance and calibration records, breakdown log, annual maintenance contracts |
| FMS.3 | Water, electricity, medical gases and vacuum are safe and available | Water testing reports, generator and UPS log, oxygen cylinder or pipeline checks, backup supply |
| FMS.4 | There are plans for fire and other emergencies | Fire plan, trained staff, displayed exit plans, emergency lighting, mock drills at least twice a year with one using simulated patients, a disaster plan |

### Chapter 9: Human Resource Management (HRM)

| Standard | In simple words | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HRM.1 | There are enough staff for patient needs | Staffing plan, duty rosters, job descriptions with minimum qualifications |
| HRM.2 | Staff are inducted and trained | Induction programme covering vision and mission, rights, CPR, infection control and safety, training calendar and attendance records, checks that training worked |
| HRM.3 | There are set procedures for appraisal, discipline and grievance | Performance appraisal forms, disciplinary and grievance procedures |
| HRM.4 | Staff health and safety are protected | Pre-employment health check, hepatitis B vaccination, needle stick injury procedure |
| HRM.5 | A personal file is kept for each staff member | Appointment letter, qualification and registration certificates, training records, health records |

Doctors and nurses must hold a valid registration with their medical or nursing council. The hospital keeps copies and checks renewal dates.

### Chapter 10: Information Management System (IMS)

| Standard | In simple words | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IMS.1 | The information needs of patients, staff, management and outside agencies are met | Reports sent to government on time, notifiable diseases reported, information boards for patients |
| IMS.2 | Every patient has a complete and accurate medical record | Records reviewed at least for timeliness, legibility and completeness, for current and discharged patients, with gaps corrected in a set time |
| IMS.3 | Records and data are kept confidential, intact and secure | Controlled access to files and software, traceable corrections, written rules on releasing records, a set retention period, a procedure for destroying old records |

The hospital decides how long to keep OPD, IPD and MLC records, following National Medical Commission and state rules. A copy of a record is released only with the patient's written permission, or where the law requires.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Infection control committee | Team that oversees infection prevention |
| Quality committee | Team that reviews incidents and indicators every month |
| KPI | Key Performance Indicator, a number tracked to measure quality |
| Organogram | Chart showing who reports to whom |
| Calibration | Checking that equipment measures correctly |
| AMC or CMC | Annual or comprehensive maintenance contract for equipment |
| Mock drill | Practice of an emergency response |
| Induction | Training given to a new staff member on joining |
| Notifiable disease | Disease that must be reported to government, such as dengue or TB |
| Retention period | How long a record must be kept |
