# Module 7: How the Standards Are Organised

The Entry Level 2nd Edition has 10 chapters, 46 standards and 189 objective elements. Every gap list, task sheet and document we prepare for a client is arranged by these chapters and codes, so learning the structure lets you find your way through any NABH file.

### Three levels

| Level | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chapter | A broad area of hospital work | Management of Medication (MOM) |
| Standard | A statement of what the hospital must achieve in that area | MOM.5: Medications are administered safely |
| Objective element (OE) | A specific point the assessor checks and scores | High risk doses are checked by two staff before they are given |

Codes are written as chapter, standard and letter. **MOM.5.c** means chapter MOM, standard 5, objective element c.

### The 10 chapters

The first five chapters are about the patient. The last five are about running the organisation.

| No. | Chapter | Code | Standards | OEs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Access, Assessment and Continuity of Care | AAC | 7 | 29 |
| 2 | Care of Patients | COP | 10 | 44 |
| 3 | Management of Medication | MOM | 7 | 28 |
| 4 | Patient Rights and Education | PRE | 2 | 17 |
| 5 | Infection Prevention and Control | IPC | 2 | 12 |
| 6 | Patient Safety and Quality Improvement | PSQ | 2 | 8 |
| 7 | Responsibility of Management | ROM | 4 | 12 |
| 8 | Facility Management and Safety | FMS | 4 | 13 |
| 9 | Human Resource Management | HRM | 5 | 15 |
| 10 | Information Management System | IMS | 3 | 11 |
|  | **Total** |  | **46** | **189** |

A simple way to remember the order: the patient arrives and is assessed (AAC), is treated (COP), gets medicines (MOM), has rights (PRE) and must be kept free of infection (IPC). Behind this the hospital measures safety (PSQ), is led well (ROM), has a safe building (FMS), has trained staff (HRM) and keeps proper records (IMS).

### Core, Commitment and Excellence

Each objective element belongs to one of three categories. A hospital does not have to meet all 189 at the first assessment. The categories are added cycle by cycle.

| Category | Number of OEs | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core | 124 | The basic safety requirements. Checked from the first assessment |
| Commitment | 36 | Requirements that show the system is being built up |
| Excellence | 29 | Higher-level requirements for mature hospitals |

| Hospital | First assessment | First renewal | Second renewal onward |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SHCO (up to 50 beds), new applicant | Core | Core + Commitment | Core + Commitment + Excellence |
| HCO (51 beds and above), new applicant | Core + Commitment | Core + Commitment + Excellence | Core + Commitment + Excellence |

A hospital renewing from an older certificate starts at Core + Commitment.

In the standards book some lines use the words can or could. These are suggestions and are not scored.

### How the assessor checks an objective element

The assessor looks for three kinds of evidence:

1. **Documents.** Is there a written policy or procedure? Is it approved and current?
2. **Records.** Is the work being done and recorded? For example, filled registers, signed forms and audit sheets.
3. **Interviews and observation.** Do staff know the procedure when asked? Does the assessor see them following it on the floor?

All three must agree. A perfect manual with empty registers fails. Staff doing the right thing with nothing written also fails.

### Policy, procedure and record

NABH documentation follows a chain.

- **Policy:** the hospital's rule or decision on a subject. Example: all patients are identified with two identifiers.
- **Procedure or SOP (Standard Operating Procedure):** the steps to follow the policy. Example: the nurse checks the patient's name and UHID on the wristband against the file before any medicine or test.
- **Form or format:** the blank sheet used to record the work. Example: the wristband and the medication chart.
- **Record:** the filled form, which is the proof. Example: the signed medication chart.

Documents that NABH requires in writing are marked with an asterisk (\*) in the standards book.

### Document control

Every official hospital document must carry:

- a name and document number
- its purpose
- dates of creation, approval and next review
- the names of the people who prepared, reviewed and approved it
- page numbers

Documents should be reviewed at least once a year. Old versions are withdrawn from use so that staff only see the current one. Records must not be altered. A correction is made by a single line through the wrong entry, with the right entry, signature and date beside it.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Chapter | One of 10 broad areas of the standards |
| Standard | What the hospital must achieve in an area |
| Objective element (OE) | Specific point that is checked and scored |
| Core, Commitment, Excellence | The three OE categories, added cycle by cycle |
| Policy | Rule or decision of the hospital |
| SOP | Standard Operating Procedure, the steps to follow |
| Format | Blank form for recording |
| Record | Filled form, used as proof |
| Document control | Rules for numbering, approving and reviewing documents |
