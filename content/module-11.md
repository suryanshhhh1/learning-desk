# Module 11: Infection Control, Biomedical Waste and Fire Safety

These three subjects come up on every hospital visit. Assessors check them on the floor, so a hospital can lose marks here even when its documents are perfect. You do not need to practise them, but you need to know what correct looks like.

### How infection spreads

Infection passes along a chain of six links:

1. **Germ:** bacteria, virus or fungus
2. **Reservoir:** where it lives, such as a patient, a wound, a dirty surface or water
3. **Exit:** how it leaves, such as through blood, cough, urine or stool
4. **Mode of transmission:** how it travels, such as by hands, air, droplets, instruments or needles
5. **Entry:** how it gets in, such as through a wound, a cannula, a catheter or the mouth
6. **Host:** a person who can be infected, especially the very old, the very young and the very sick

Infection control breaks one or more links. Hand hygiene breaks the mode of transmission. Sterilisation removes the germ. Vaccination protects the host.

### Standard precautions

Standard precautions apply to every patient, whatever their illness:

- hand hygiene
- gloves, gown, mask and eye protection when there is a chance of contact with blood or body fluids
- safe use and disposal of needles and sharps
- cleaning of equipment and surfaces
- cough etiquette
- safe handling of linen and waste

Patients with infections that spread by air or contact, such as TB or resistant bacteria, also need isolation precautions, for example a separate room and an N95 mask.

### Hand hygiene

The WHO 5 moments for hand hygiene are:

1. before touching a patient
2. before a clean or aseptic procedure
3. after exposure to body fluids
4. after touching a patient
5. after touching the patient's surroundings

An alcohol hand rub takes 20 to 30 seconds. Washing with soap and water takes 40 to 60 seconds and is used when hands are visibly dirty. Both follow the same steps covering palms, backs, fingers, thumbs and fingertips.

### Hospital acquired infections (HAI)

An HAI is an infection that the patient did not have on admission and caught in the hospital. The four that hospitals track are:

| Infection | Linked to |
| --- | --- |
| CAUTI (Catheter Associated Urinary Tract Infection) | Urine catheter |
| CLABSI (Central Line Associated Bloodstream Infection) | Central IV line |
| VAP (Ventilator Associated Pneumonia) | Ventilator |
| SSI (Surgical Site Infection) | Surgical wound |

Each has a prevention bundle, a short set of steps done every time, such as removing the catheter as soon as it is no longer needed.

### Needle stick injury

If a staff member is pricked by a used needle:

1. Wash the area with soap and running water. Do not squeeze or suck it.
2. Report it at once to the in-charge and fill an incident form.
3. The patient's and the staff member's blood are tested for HIV, hepatitis B and hepatitis C.
4. The doctor decides on preventive medicine, which works best when started within a few hours.
5. Follow-up tests are done over the next months.

Needles are never recapped by hand, and sharps go straight into the sharps container.

### Biomedical waste (BMW)

Biomedical waste is handled under the Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016. Waste is separated at the point where it is made, into colour coded bins and bags.

| Colour | What goes in | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yellow | Infectious and anatomical waste, soiled items, expired medicines, chemical and cytotoxic waste | Body parts, dressings soaked with blood, plaster casts, discarded tablets, chemotherapy waste |
| Red | Contaminated plastic that can be recycled | IV sets, catheters, urine bags, syringes without needles, gloves |
| White (translucent, puncture proof) | Sharps | Needles, blades, scalpels, syringes with fixed needles |
| Blue (box or container) | Glass and metal implants | Broken vials, ampoules, glass slides, metal implants |
| Black | General waste that is not biomedical | Paper, food waste, packaging |

Other rules to know:

- Bags are labelled with the biohazard symbol and barcoded.
- Waste is stored in a locked area and handed to an authorised Common Biomedical Waste Treatment Facility, normally within 48 hours.
- The hospital needs BMW authorisation from the State Pollution Control Board and must file an annual report.
- Staff handling waste are trained and vaccinated against hepatitis B.

### Fire safety

Staff follow two short rules when they find a fire.

**RACE:** Rescue anyone in danger, raise the Alarm, Contain the fire by closing doors, Extinguish it if it is small or Evacuate.

**PASS**, for using an extinguisher: Pull the pin, Aim at the base of the fire, Squeeze the handle, Sweep from side to side.

| Extinguisher | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| ABC dry powder | Most fires, including wood, paper, fuel and electrical fires |
| CO2 (carbon dioxide) | Electrical fires and equipment, as it leaves no residue |
| Water | Wood, paper and cloth only. Never on electrical or oil fires |

A compliant hospital has extinguishers checked and tagged, clear fire exits, lit exit signs, evacuation plans on the walls, a valid fire NOC, trained staff and records of mock drills.

### Emergency codes

Hospitals use colour codes to call teams without alarming patients. The exact list varies by hospital. Common ones are:

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Code Red | Fire |
| Code Blue | Cardiac or breathing arrest |
| Code Pink | Infant or child abduction |
| Code Brown | Hazardous spill |

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Standard precautions | Infection control steps used for every patient |
| PPE | Personal Protective Equipment: gloves, gown, mask, goggles |
| HAI | Hospital Acquired Infection |
| Bundle | Short set of prevention steps done together every time |
| NSI | Needle Stick Injury |
| BMW | Biomedical Waste |
| CBWTF | Common Biomedical Waste Treatment Facility |
| RACE and PASS | Fire response and extinguisher steps |
| Fire NOC | No Objection Certificate from the fire department |
