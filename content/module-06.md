# Module 6: What NABH Is

NABH is the national body that checks whether a hospital follows a fixed set of quality and safety standards. A hospital that meets them receives a certificate or accreditation for a fixed period. Most of our clients are applying for the Entry Level Certification, 2nd Edition, which came into effect in January 2026.

### Who runs NABH

NABH stands for National Accreditation Board for Hospitals and Healthcare Providers. It is a board of the Quality Council of India (QCI). QCI is an autonomous body set up by the Government of India together with industry associations to promote quality in all sectors. Other QCI boards cover laboratories, education and industry.

### Licence, registration and accreditation

These three words are often mixed up. They are different things.

|  | Licence or registration | NABH certification or accreditation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Given by | Government departments, such as the state health department, pollution board or fire department | NABH, under QCI |
| Is it compulsory? | Yes. A hospital cannot run legally without it | No. The hospital applies by choice |
| What it checks | That the hospital meets the legal minimum | That the hospital follows quality and safety systems every day |
| Examples | Clinical establishment registration, fire NOC, BMW authorisation | Entry Level Certification, Full Accreditation |

NABH expects every licence to be valid. A hospital with an expired licence will not get certified. Module 12 covers the licences in detail.

### Programmes NABH runs

NABH has many programmes. The ones you will hear about most are:

- **Entry Level Certification for hospitals:** the first step, with a smaller set of standards. This is our main work.
- **Full Accreditation for hospitals:** the complete set of standards, now in its 6th edition, with several hundred objective elements.
- **Small Healthcare Organisation (SHCO) accreditation:** for small hospitals under the full programme.
- **Other programmes:** medical laboratories, nursing excellence, emergency departments, blood banks, dental, AYUSH hospitals, eye care and digital health.

### Entry Level 2nd Edition at a glance

- One set of standards applies to all hospitals. Hospitals of up to 50 beds are called SHCOs, and hospitals of 51 beds and above are called HCOs.
- Hospitals above 300 beds cannot apply under Entry Level.
- Only allopathic (modern medicine) services are covered.
- The hospital must have been working for at least 6 months, and must have followed the NABH standards for at least 3 months, before it applies.
- Bed occupancy must be at least 20 percent in the last 3 months before the final assessment. It must be 25 percent for the first renewal and 30 percent for the second renewal. Day care beds count.
- The all or none rule applies. Every service the hospital offers must be included in the application. A hospital cannot leave out a weak department.

### The certification process

1. **Prepare.** The hospital writes its policies and procedures, trains staff and starts keeping records. This is where our consultancy work happens.
2. **Run the system.** The hospital follows the standards for at least 3 months and keeps the records to prove it.
3. **Register and apply online.** The application lists the hospital's services, beds and licences, with documents uploaded on the NABH portal.
4. **Document review.** NABH checks the application and may ask for corrections.
5. **Assessment.** NABH assessors visit the hospital. They read documents, check records, talk to staff and watch how work is done.
6. **Non-conformities.** Gaps found by the assessors are listed. The hospital closes them with proof, within a set time.
7. **Decision and certificate.** NABH reviews the report and awards the certificate.
8. **Renewal.** The hospital applies again before the certificate ends. Each renewal cycle adds more objective elements, as Module 7 explains.

### Fees

The certification fee covers two years. GST at 18 percent is added.

| Beds | Certification fee for 2 years |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 5 | Rs 21,000 |
| 6 to 20 | Rs 40,000 |
| 21 to 50 | Rs 80,000 |
| 51 to 100 | Rs 1,60,000 |
| 101 to 300 | Rs 2,00,000 |

A focus assessment, an extra visit to check specific gaps, costs Rs 15,000. Re-issue of a certificate costs Rs 6,000. Check the NABH website before quoting fees to a client, as they can change.

### Why hospitals apply

- **Insurance:** many insurers and government schemes pay higher rates to NABH hospitals or require it for empanelment. Module 13 covers this.
- **Patient trust:** the certificate shows patients that the hospital follows checked standards.
- **Fewer errors:** written procedures, checks and reporting reduce mistakes in care.
- **Better management:** the hospital gets data on infections, errors and complaints and can act on it.
- **Legal protection:** good records protect staff and the hospital if a case is challenged.

### Help from NABH

NABH runs E-Mitra, a free support kit with sample policies, procedures, checklists, forms and registers for Entry Level hospitals. It also runs free training programmes on implementing the standards.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| QCI | Quality Council of India |
| NABH | National Accreditation Board for Hospitals and Healthcare Providers |
| Accreditation | Formal recognition that an organisation meets a full set of standards |
| Certification | Formal recognition under a smaller programme, such as Entry Level |
| SHCO | Small Healthcare Organisation, up to 50 beds |
| HCO | Healthcare Organisation, 51 beds and above |
| Assessor | Person sent by NABH to check the hospital |
| Non-conformity (NC) | A gap found by the assessor that must be closed |
| Bed occupancy | Share of beds used by patients over a period |
| All or none | Every service must be included in the application |
