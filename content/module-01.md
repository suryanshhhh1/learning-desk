# Module 1: How a Hospital Works

A hospital is a group of departments, and each department does one job. A patient passes through several of them in a single visit. If you know the departments and the people in them, you can follow any hospital document you read.

### Types of healthcare facilities

- **Clinic:** a doctor sees patients and they go home the same day. There are no beds for admission.
- **Nursing home or small hospital:** has beds for admitted patients, usually a small number. Common services are general medicine, surgery and maternity.
- **Multispeciality hospital:** many specialities under one roof, such as medicine, surgery, orthopaedics, gynaecology and paediatrics.
- **Single speciality or super speciality hospital:** one field only, for example eye care, fertility, cardiology or neurology.
- **Diagnostic centre or laboratory:** only tests and scans. No treatment or admission.

Hospitals are also grouped as primary, secondary and tertiary care. Primary care is the first contact, such as a clinic or health centre. Secondary care is a district-level hospital with common specialities. Tertiary care is a large hospital with advanced specialities and critical care.

### Departments and what they do

| Department | What happens there |
| --- | --- |
| Reception or front office | Patient registration, enquiries, appointments |
| OPD (Outpatient Department) | Patient meets the doctor and goes home the same day |
| IPD (Inpatient Department) | Patient is admitted and stays in a bed |
| Emergency or Casualty | Urgent cases, open 24 hours |
| Ward and rooms | Where admitted patients stay and are cared for |
| ICU (Intensive Care Unit) | Very sick patients who need constant monitoring |
| OT (Operation Theatre) | Surgeries and procedures |
| Laboratory or Pathology | Blood, urine and other sample tests |
| Radiology | X-ray, ultrasound (USG), CT scan, MRI |
| Pharmacy | Stores and gives out medicines |
| Billing and TPA desk | Bills, insurance approvals, claim papers |
| MRD (Medical Records Department) | Stores and issues patient files |
| CSSD (Central Sterile Supply Department) | Cleans and sterilises instruments and linen used in surgery |
| Housekeeping, laundry, kitchen, security, maintenance | Support services that keep the building running |
| Quality department | Audits, incident reports, accreditation work |

### Who is who

| Role | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Consultant | Senior doctor in one speciality. Decides diagnosis and treatment |
| Visiting consultant | Consultant who is not full time and attends the hospital on fixed days or when called |
| RMO (Resident Medical Officer) | Doctor present in the hospital round the clock. First doctor to attend a ward or emergency call |
| Surgeon | Doctor who performs operations |
| Anaesthetist | Doctor who gives anaesthesia and watches the patient during surgery |
| Intensivist | Doctor who manages ICU patients |
| Radiologist | Doctor who reads X-rays, scans and ultrasounds and writes the report |
| Pathologist | Doctor who oversees the laboratory and signs test reports |
| Staff nurse | Gives medicines, monitors the patient, writes nursing notes |
| Ward in-charge or nursing superintendent | Senior nurse who runs a ward or the whole nursing team |
| Technician | Runs equipment or tests. Lab, OT, radiology and dialysis each have their own |
| Pharmacist | Manages medicine stock and gives medicines against a prescription |
| Physiotherapist and dietician | Help recovery through exercise and diet planning |
| Medical superintendent or director | Head of the clinical side of the hospital |
| Hospital administrator and quality manager | Run operations, licences, records and compliance |

### The journey of a patient

This is the path of a patient who is admitted. Day patients stop after step 4.

1. **Arrival and registration.** The reception creates a file and gives the patient a UHID. A returning patient is found in the system by name or phone number.
2. **OPD consultation.** The doctor asks about the problem, examines the patient and decides which tests are needed.
3. **Investigations.** The patient goes to the laboratory or radiology. Reports come back to the doctor.
4. **Diagnosis and advice.** The doctor either prescribes medicines and sends the patient home, or advises admission.
5. **Admission.** An admission form and consent are signed, a bed is allotted, and for cashless insurance the TPA desk sends a pre-authorisation request.
6. **Treatment.** The patient is in a ward, ICU or OT. Doctors visit, nurses give medicines, and every step is written in the patient file.
7. **Discharge planning.** The doctor decides the patient is fit to leave. The final bill is made and the final approval is taken from the insurer.
8. **Discharge.** The patient receives a discharge summary, medicines and a follow-up date.
9. **Records and claim.** The file goes to the MRD. The billing and TPA desk submits the claim with the required papers.

In an emergency the order changes. The patient is treated first and registration follows once the patient is stable.

**Where your work sits:** steps 5 and 9. The pre-authorisation and the claim both depend on what the doctors and nurses wrote in steps 2 to 8.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| OPD | Outpatient Department. Visit and go home |
| IPD | Inpatient Department. Admitted patients |
| ICU | Intensive Care Unit |
| OT | Operation Theatre |
| RMO | Resident Medical Officer |
| MRD | Medical Records Department |
| CSSD | Central Sterile Supply Department |
| TPA | Third Party Administrator. Handles insurance claims for the insurer |
| Pre-authorisation | Approval from the insurer before a cashless admission or procedure |
| Speciality | A branch of medicine, such as cardiology or orthopaedics |
