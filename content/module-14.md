# Module 14: Reading a Hospital

This module is practical. You will visit a client hospital with a senior team member, walk through it with a checklist, and trace one patient file from admission to claim. The aim is to recognise on the floor what you have read in Modules 1 to 13.

### Before the visit

- Read the hospital's scope of services and bed count.
- Look at its licence tracker and note any licence close to expiry.
- Carry a printed copy of the walkthrough checklist below.
- Do not touch patients, open patient files without permission, or photograph patients. Patient privacy applies to us as visitors too.

### Walkthrough checklist

Tick what you see. Write a short note beside anything missing.

**Reception and front office**

- [ ] Scope of services board displayed
- [ ] Patient rights and responsibilities displayed in the local language
- [ ] Tariff or estimate process available
- [ ] Registration creates a UHID
- [ ] Complaint or feedback box or process visible
- [ ] Registration certificate and other licences displayed where required

**Emergency**

- [ ] Triage area or process
- [ ] Crash cart, with a checklist filled every day
- [ ] MLC register
- [ ] Code Blue process known to staff

**Wards and ICU**

- [ ] Patients wear identification bands with two identifiers
- [ ] Hand rub at every bed or patient area
- [ ] Vitals charts and medication charts up to date
- [ ] Fall risk assessment in the file for high risk patients
- [ ] Fridge temperature chart filled every day
- [ ] High alert and LASA medicines stored apart and labelled

**Operation theatre and CSSD**

- [ ] Zones marked and followed
- [ ] Surgical safety checklist in use
- [ ] Sterilisation indicators recorded
- [ ] Implant register maintained

**Pharmacy**

- [ ] Drug licence and pharmacist registration displayed
- [ ] Expiry and near expiry stock separated
- [ ] Narcotic medicines double locked, with a register

**Laboratory and radiology**

- [ ] Sample labelling with two identifiers
- [ ] Critical value register
- [ ] AERB licence, radiation warning signs and lead aprons
- [ ] PCPNDT certificate displayed in the ultrasound room

**Support areas**

- [ ] Colour coded biomedical waste bins in every area
- [ ] Waste storage area locked and labelled
- [ ] Fire extinguishers tagged with a valid date
- [ ] Fire exits clear and exit signs lit
- [ ] Evacuation plan displayed
- [ ] Generator and oxygen logs maintained

**Office areas**

- [ ] Staff files with qualifications and registrations
- [ ] Training calendar and attendance records
- [ ] Incident register
- [ ] KPI register and quality committee minutes
- [ ] MRD with controlled access and a record release register

### File tracing exercise

Ask the MRD for one discharged insurance patient's file, with permission. Answer these questions from the file alone:

1. What are the UHID and IP number? Are they the same on every page?
2. What were the complaints at admission, and when was the initial assessment done?
3. What was the provisional diagnosis, and what was the final diagnosis?
4. Which tests were done, and which results supported the diagnosis?
5. What procedure or surgery was done? Is there an operation note and a signed consent?
6. Pick three medicines from the bill. Can you find each one in the doctor's orders and the medication chart?
7. Does the discharge summary match the case sheet on dates, diagnosis and procedure?
8. If you were the TPA, what query would you raise?

Write your answers on one page and discuss them with your reviewer.

### Final review test

Answer these after the visit. They cover all modules.

1. What is the difference between a UHID and an IP number?
2. Read this order in words: Inj. Pantoprazole 40 mg IV OD.
3. What is the difference between IV and IM?
4. What does LAMA mean, and what document is signed?
5. Name the three steps of the surgical safety checklist.
6. What do the letters NABH stand for, and under which body does it work?
7. How many chapters, standards and objective elements are in the Entry Level 2nd Edition?
8. Which category of objective elements is checked at the first assessment of a new 30-bed hospital?
9. Which chapter covers medicine storage, and which standard covers implants?
10. Give one example each of a near miss, an adverse event and a sentinel event.
11. What is CAPA?
12. Which bin takes broken glass vials?
13. Name four licences every hospital needs.
14. How does NABH Entry Level certification change a hospital's PM-JAY package rates?
15. Name three TPA queries that a good NABH system prevents.

The glossary tables at the end of each module together form your full reference list of terms.
