# Module 3: Medical Language

Medical documents use a fixed vocabulary and many short forms. Once you know how a doctor reaches a diagnosis and learn about 60 terms, most discharge summaries and claim papers become readable.

### How a doctor reaches a diagnosis

1. **Complaints.** The patient states the problem in their own words, for example fever for three days.
2. **History.** The doctor asks how the problem started, what illnesses the patient has had before, what runs in the family, what medicines the patient takes and whether the patient has any allergy.
3. **Examination.** The doctor checks the patient. This begins with vitals and continues with the part of the body that is affected.
4. **Provisional diagnosis.** The doctor's first opinion on what the problem is. It can change after tests.
5. **Investigations.** Tests and scans ordered to confirm or rule out the opinion.
6. **Final diagnosis.** The confirmed condition. This is the diagnosis written in the discharge summary and used by the insurer.
7. **Treatment plan.** Medicines, procedure or surgery, diet and rest.
8. **Follow-up.** A review visit to check recovery.

### Vitals

Vitals are the basic body readings taken by the nurse at admission and at fixed intervals after that. Values below are for a healthy adult and are approximate. The doctor decides what is normal for each patient.

| Vital | What it measures | Usual adult range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temperature | Body heat | About 97 to 99 degrees F (36.1 to 37.2 degrees C) |
| Pulse or heart rate (HR) | Heartbeats per minute | 60 to 100 per minute |
| Blood pressure (BP) | Pressure of blood in the arteries. Written as upper over lower number | About 120/80 mmHg |
| Respiratory rate (RR) | Breaths per minute | 12 to 20 per minute |
| SpO2 | Oxygen level in the blood, measured by a clip on the finger | 95 to 100 percent |
| Blood sugar (RBS or GRBS) | Sugar level in the blood, measured by a finger prick | Depends on timing of the last meal |

### Common tests

| Test | What it checks |
| --- | --- |
| CBC (Complete Blood Count) | Haemoglobin, white cells, platelets. Shows anaemia, infection, bleeding risk |
| Blood sugar (fasting, post meal, random) and HbA1c | Diabetes and its control over three months |
| LFT (Liver Function Test) | Liver health |
| KFT or RFT (Kidney or Renal Function Test) | Kidney health. Includes urea and creatinine |
| Serum electrolytes | Sodium, potassium and other salts in blood |
| Urine routine and microscopy | Infection, sugar, protein and blood in urine |
| Culture and sensitivity | Grows germs from a sample to find which antibiotic works |
| ECG (Electrocardiogram) | Electrical activity of the heart |
| 2D Echo | Ultrasound of the heart |
| X-ray | Picture of bones and chest |
| USG (Ultrasonography) | Sound wave picture of the abdomen, pelvis, pregnancy and other soft parts |
| CT scan | Detailed cross section pictures using X-rays |
| MRI | Detailed pictures using magnets. Best for brain, spine and joints |

### Word parts

Most medical terms are built from a few roots, prefixes and endings. Learn these and you can guess the meaning of a new word.

| Part | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -itis | inflammation | Appendicitis, inflammation of the appendix |
| -ectomy | removal by surgery | Appendectomy, removal of the appendix |
| -otomy | cutting open | Laparotomy, opening the abdomen |
| -ostomy | making an opening | Colostomy, an opening of the bowel on the abdomen |
| -plasty | repair or reshaping | Hernioplasty, repair of a hernia |
| -scopy | looking inside with a camera | Endoscopy, Laparoscopy |
| -gram or -graphy | a recording or picture | Angiography, picture of blood vessels |
| -emia | in the blood | Anaemia, low haemoglobin |
| cardio | heart | Cardiology |
| nephro or renal | kidney | Nephrology |
| hepato | liver | Hepatitis |
| gastro | stomach and gut | Gastroenteritis |
| neuro | nerves and brain | Neurology |
| ortho | bones and joints | Orthopaedics |
| pulmo or pneumo | lungs | Pneumonia |
| derma | skin | Dermatology |
| haem | blood | Haematology |
| onco | tumour or cancer | Oncology |

### Reading a prescription

A prescription is the doctor's written order for medicines. Look for these parts in order:

1. Patient name, age, sex and date
2. Diagnosis or complaints
3. Each medicine with its strength, form (tablet, syrup, injection), how much to take, how often and for how many days
4. Advice such as diet or rest
5. Review date
6. Doctor's name, registration number and signature

The dose, frequency and route columns are explained in Module 4.
