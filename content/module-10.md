# Module 10: Quality and Safety Tools

A NABH hospital finds its own mistakes, studies why they happened and fixes the cause. It does this with a small set of tools: incident reporting, root cause analysis, CAPA, indicators, audits and patient feedback. Assessors ask staff about these tools more than anything else.

### Incidents

An incident is any event that harmed a patient, visitor or staff member, or could have. NABH asks hospitals to classify each one.

| Type | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Near miss | An event that could have caused harm but did not | A nurse picks up the wrong injection, notices before giving it and corrects it |
| Adverse event | Harm caused by the care itself, apart from the illness | A patient falls from a bed without side rails and breaks a wrist |
| Sentinel event | A rare, unexpected event that causes death or serious lasting harm and points to a system failure | Surgery on the wrong side of the body, or a death from a wrong blood transfusion |

Each hospital writes its own list of sentinel events. Common entries are wrong patient or wrong site surgery, a swab left inside a patient, death from a medication error, an infant abduction and a patient suicide in the hospital.

### How incident reporting works

1. Any staff member who sees or causes an incident fills an incident report form.
2. The report reaches the quality manager within the time set by the hospital, usually within 24 hours. A sentinel event is reported at once.
3. The quality team grades its severity and decides how deeply to study it.
4. The cause is found and corrected.
5. The quality committee reviews all incidents at its monthly meeting.

Reporting is meant to fix systems. A hospital that punishes people for reporting ends up with fewer reports and the same errors. Assessors are wary of a hospital that shows an empty incident register, because every hospital has incidents.

### Root cause analysis (RCA)

RCA means asking why something happened until the real cause is found. Two simple methods are used.

**Five whys.** Keep asking why.

- A patient got the wrong medicine. Why? The nurse picked the wrong vial.
- Why? Two vials looked almost the same.
- Why were they mixed up? They were kept in the same tray.
- Why? There was no rule to store LASA drugs apart.
- Why? The hospital had no LASA list.

The root cause is the missing LASA list and storage rule. Blaming the nurse would not stop it happening again.

**Fishbone diagram.** Possible causes are sorted under headings such as people, process, equipment, materials and environment, so that no area is missed.

### CAPA

CAPA stands for Corrective Action and Preventive Action.

- **Corrective action** fixes the problem that happened. Example: separate the two vials and label them.
- **Preventive action** stops the same or a similar problem from happening anywhere. Example: make a LASA list for the whole hospital, store all LASA drugs apart and train every nurse.

Each CAPA names a responsible person and a deadline, and someone checks later that it was done.

### Key performance indicators (KPIs)

A KPI is a number the hospital tracks every month to see whether care is getting better or worse. Each indicator has a numerator (the events counted), a denominator (the total they are counted out of) and a multiplier.

**Example: hand hygiene compliance**

Hand hygiene compliance rate = (hand hygiene actions done / opportunities observed) x 100

If an auditor watches 50 moments where hands should be cleaned and staff cleaned them in 42, the rate is 42 / 50 x 100 = 84 percent.

| Common indicators | What they show |
| --- | --- |
| Bed occupancy rate | How full the hospital is |
| Average length of stay | How long patients stay on average |
| Medication error rate | Errors per 1,000 medication orders or doses |
| Patient fall rate | Falls per 1,000 patient days |
| Hospital acquired infection rates (CAUTI, CLABSI, VAP, SSI) | Infections caught in the hospital |
| Return to ICU within 48 hours | Whether patients were moved out of ICU too early |
| Re-intubation rate | Breathing tubes put back after removal |
| Time to initial assessment | How fast a doctor sees a newly admitted patient |
| Patient satisfaction score | What patients think of the care |
| Waiting time in OPD | How long patients wait to see a doctor |

NABH also expects hospitals to track any indicator the government asks for.

### Audits

An audit is a planned check of whether a procedure is being followed. Examples are hand hygiene audits, medication order audits (at least monthly), medical record audits, biomedical waste audits, and surgical safety checklist audits. Each audit has a checklist, a sample, a score, the gaps found and the CAPA.

### Patient feedback and complaints

The hospital collects feedback on paper or electronically. Complaints, including verbal and phone complaints, are logged, looked into within a set time and answered. Patients are told how to complain. Repeated complaints on one subject lead to a CAPA.

### Risk assessment

The hospital lists the risks in each department, rates how likely and how serious each one is, and puts controls in place for the highest. A fall risk score for each patient is a small version of the same idea.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Incident | Event that caused or could have caused harm |
| Near miss | Incident caught before it caused harm |
| Adverse event | Harm caused by medical care |
| Sentinel event | Rare event causing death or serious harm, pointing to a system failure |
| RCA | Root Cause Analysis |
| CAPA | Corrective and Preventive Action |
| KPI | Key Performance Indicator |
| Numerator and denominator | The top and bottom numbers of a rate |
| Audit | Planned check of whether a procedure is followed |
| Patient days | Total days spent in hospital by all patients in a period |
