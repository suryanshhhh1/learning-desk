# Module 5: Surgery, Anaesthesia and Critical Care

Surgery and ICU care carry the highest risk in a hospital and generate the largest claims. This module explains what happens in the operation theatre, the types of anaesthesia, the terms used in the ICU, and how instruments are made safe for use.

### The path of a surgical patient

1. **Before surgery.** The patient is examined and tests are done. The anaesthetist checks fitness for anaesthesia in a pre-anaesthetic check (PAC). Informed consent is signed for the surgery and the anaesthesia. The patient is told to stay nil by mouth for several hours before surgery.
2. **Shifting to the OT.** The nurse checks the patient's name, UHID, consent, surgical site and allergy before the patient enters the theatre.
3. **In the OT.** The anaesthetist gives anaesthesia, the surgeon operates, and the nurse counts instruments and swabs.
4. **Recovery.** The patient is watched in a recovery area until fully awake and stable.
5. **Back to ward or ICU.** The surgeon writes post-operative orders for pain relief, fluids, diet and medicines.

### The OT team

| Role | Task |
| --- | --- |
| Surgeon | Performs the operation |
| Assistant surgeon | Helps the surgeon |
| Anaesthetist | Puts the patient to sleep or numbs the body, and monitors the patient throughout |
| Scrub nurse | Wears sterile gear, stands at the table and hands instruments |
| Circulating nurse | Not sterile. Brings supplies, records counts and keeps the notes |
| OT technician | Prepares equipment and the theatre |

### The OT is divided into zones

- **Outer zone:** reception of the OT, changing room, store. Anyone in hospital dress can enter.
- **Clean zone:** corridor to the theatres. OT dress, cap and mask are required.
- **Sterile zone:** the operating room itself. Only the team enters.

The air in the OT is filtered, doors stay closed during surgery, and footwear and dress are changed at the entry. These controls are there to prevent infection in the wound.

### Surgical safety checklist

The WHO surgical safety checklist is filled in three steps:

- **Sign in:** before anaesthesia, the team confirms the patient's identity, consent, site and allergy.
- **Time out:** before the first cut, everyone stops and confirms the patient, procedure and site together.
- **Sign out:** before the patient leaves, the team confirms the procedure done, the instrument and swab counts and the labelling of any specimen.

### Types of surgery

- **Minor and major:** a minor surgery is a small procedure such as stitching a cut. A major surgery involves a large operation, deeper tissue or longer anaesthesia.
- **Elective and emergency:** elective surgery is planned in advance. Emergency surgery is done at once to save life or limb.
- **Open, laparoscopic and endoscopic:** open surgery uses one larger cut. Laparoscopic surgery, also called keyhole surgery, uses small cuts and a camera. Endoscopic procedures go through a natural opening such as the mouth.

### Types of anaesthesia

| Type | What happens | Common use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General anaesthesia (GA) | The patient is fully asleep and breathing is supported | Major and long operations |
| Spinal anaesthesia | An injection in the lower back numbs the body below the waist. The patient stays awake | Caesarean section, lower limb and lower abdomen surgery |
| Epidural | A thin tube is placed near the spine to give medicine over time | Labour pain, some major surgeries |
| Regional or nerve block | A nerve supplying one limb is numbed | Arm or leg surgery |
| Local anaesthesia | A small area is numbed by an injection | Stitches, small cuts, cataract |
| Sedation | The patient is relaxed or drowsy | Scopy procedures |

The anaesthetist also records an ASA grade, a number from I to V that describes how fit the patient is for anaesthesia. Grade I is a healthy patient and higher grades show more illness.

### The operation note

The surgeon writes this note after surgery. It records the date, the team, the diagnosis before and after surgery, the procedure done, the findings, the blood loss, any implant used and any complication. It is a key document for the claim.

### Implants

An implant is a device that stays in the body, for example a stent, a joint replacement, a plate and screws, an intraocular lens or a mesh. The hospital keeps the sticker or barcode label of each implant in the file, and the insurer asks for it together with the implant invoice.

### Blood bank and transfusion

Blood is stored in a blood bank or blood storage unit. Before a transfusion, the patient's blood is matched with the donor blood in a test called cross matching. Written consent is taken for transfusion, and the nurse watches the patient for reactions.

### Intensive care

The ICU treats patients whose organs may fail. The terms below appear in ICU notes and bills.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| ICCU or CCU | Intensive care for heart patients |
| NICU | Newborn ICU |
| PICU | Paediatric ICU, for children |
| HDU | High Dependency Unit, for patients who need close watch but less than ICU |
| Monitor | Screen showing heart rhythm, pulse, blood pressure and oxygen level |
| Ventilator | Machine that breathes for the patient |
| Intubation | Placing a tube through the mouth into the windpipe for ventilation |
| Tracheostomy | Opening in the neck for a breathing tube, used when ventilation is long |
| Oxygen support | Given through nasal prongs, a mask or a ventilator |
| Syringe pump or infusion pump | Machine that gives a medicine at a steady rate |
| Central line | Long IV line placed in a large vein, for strong medicines and monitoring |
| Foley's catheter | Tube to drain urine |
| Ryle's tube | Tube through the nose into the stomach, for feeding or drainage |
| Dialysis | Machine cleaning of blood when the kidneys fail |
| GCS (Glasgow Coma Scale) | Score of 3 to 15 showing how alert the patient is |
| CPR | Cardiopulmonary resuscitation, chest compressions and breathing support when the heart stops |
| Crash cart | Emergency trolley with drugs and equipment for resuscitation |
| Code Blue | The hospital's call for a patient whose heart or breathing has stopped |

### Cleaning, disinfection and sterilisation

These three words mean different levels of germ removal.

| Level | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleaning | Removes dirt and visible matter with soap and water | Washing instruments after use |
| Disinfection | Kills most germs but not all | Wiping a bed with a disinfectant, or soaking a scope |
| Sterilisation | Kills all germs including their spores | Autoclaving surgical instruments |

The common sterilisation methods are:

- **Autoclave:** steam under pressure. This is the most widely used method for metal instruments and linen.
- **ETO (ethylene oxide) or plasma:** used for items that cannot take heat, such as plastics and cameras.

The CSSD works in one direction, from dirty to clean. Used instruments arrive at the dirty end, are cleaned, packed, sterilised and stored at the clean end. Indicators are used to prove that sterilisation worked. A chemical indicator on each pack changes colour when conditions are reached, and a biological test with spores is run at regular intervals.
