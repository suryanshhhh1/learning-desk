# Module 8: NABH Chapters in Plain Language, Part 1

This module walks through the four chapters that follow the patient: AAC, COP, MOM and PRE. Together they hold 118 of the 189 objective elements. For each standard you get its meaning in simple words and what you will actually see in a compliant hospital.

### Chapter 1: Access, Assessment and Continuity of Care (AAC)

This chapter covers the patient's path from arrival to discharge, along with the laboratory and imaging services used on the way.

| Standard | In simple words | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AAC.1 | The hospital states and displays the services it provides | A scope of services board at reception, and patients turned away or referred if a service is not available |
| AAC.2 | Registration, admission and transfer follow a set process | UHID system, admission form, written transfer and referral procedure, a policy for when beds are full |
| AAC.3 | Every patient gets an initial assessment | Doctor and nurse assessment forms filled within a set time after admission |
| AAC.4 | Care continues with regular reassessment | Daily doctor notes, vitals charts, a proper handover between shifts |
| AAC.5 | The laboratory works within its scope and follows lab safety | Lab licence, sample collection and labelling procedure, critical value reporting, quality control records |
| AAC.6 | Imaging follows the radiation safety programme | AERB licence, radiation badges for staff, lead aprons, warning signs |
| AAC.7 | Discharge follows a set process | Discharge summary for every patient, a LAMA or DAMA form, a death summary where needed |

### Chapter 2: Care of Patients (COP)

COP is the largest chapter, with 10 standards and 44 objective elements. It asks the hospital to give the same standard of care to every patient, guided by written procedures.

| Standard | In simple words | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| COP.1 | Written guidance is used so that care is the same for everyone | Clinical SOPs and protocols in each department |
| COP.2 | Emergency and ambulance services follow written guidance and the law | Triage system, MLC register, ambulance checklist, trained ambulance staff |
| COP.3 | ICU and HDU run in a systematic way | Admission and discharge criteria for ICU, staffing rules, ICU quality indicators |
| COP.4 | Obstetric (pregnancy and delivery) care is safe | Antenatal records, labour room procedures, high risk pregnancy identification |
| COP.5 | Care of children and newborns is safe | Newborn identification, measures against child abduction, paediatric drug dosing by weight |
| COP.6 | Sedation is given safely | Consent, monitoring during sedation, recovery criteria |
| COP.7 | Anaesthesia is given safely | Pre-anaesthetic check, anaesthesia record, recording of adverse anaesthesia events |
| COP.8 | Surgery and procedures are done safely | Surgical safety checklist, site marking, operation notes, list of who may perform which procedure |
| COP.9 | High risk patients are identified and managed | Fall risk and pressure sore assessment, restraint procedure, Code Blue team and CPR records |
| COP.10 | Pain, rehabilitation and nutrition are managed | Pain score on the chart, physiotherapy notes, diet orders |

### Chapter 3: Management of Medication (MOM)

MOM covers a medicine from the moment it is bought to the moment it is given. Module 4 gave you the vocabulary.

| Standard | In simple words | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MOM.1 | The pharmacy runs safely and medicines are used safely | Pharmacy licence, qualified pharmacist, a hospital formulary |
| MOM.2 | Medicines are stored properly and are available where needed | Fridge temperature charts, expiry checks, LASA and high alert medicines listed and stored apart, emergency medicines in each area |
| MOM.3 | Prescriptions follow written guidance | Rules for writing orders, legible orders with date, time and signature, a monthly audit of medication orders |
| MOM.4 | Dispensing follows written guidance | The pharmacist checks the order before issuing, labels medicines and recalls faulty batches |
| MOM.5 | Medicines are given safely | The nurse checks the order and the patient before giving, two staff check high risk doses, every dose is recorded, medication errors and adverse reactions are reported |
| MOM.6 | Narcotics, psychotropics, chemotherapy and radioactive drugs are handled safely | Double-locked storage, narcotic register, chemotherapy given by trained staff only |
| MOM.7 | Implants and medical devices are used by set rules | Implant register, implant stickers in the file, recall procedure |

Nurses check several rights before every dose. The basic five are patient, drug, dose, route and time. Most hospitals train on a longer list that adds the right documentation, the right reason, the right response, the patient's right to refuse and patient education.

### Chapter 4: Patient Rights and Education (PRE)

PRE has only 2 standards but 17 objective elements.

| Standard | In simple words | What you will see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PRE.1 | Patient rights are written, displayed and respect the patient's beliefs | Patient rights and responsibilities board in the local language, privacy during examination, a complaint process, confidentiality of information, an estimate of cost given before treatment |
| PRE.2 | Patients and families are taught enough to make their own decisions and take part in care | Informed consent forms, education records on medicines, diet and care at home, patients told about their condition and plan |

Common patient rights include the right to information about the illness and treatment, to know the expected cost, to privacy and dignity, to confidentiality, to give or refuse consent, to a second opinion, to complain and to have the complaint answered, and to receive a copy of their records.

### What this means for your work

When a hospital's records go to an insurer, much of what the insurer reads comes from these chapters: the initial assessment (AAC.3), the progress notes (AAC.4), the operation notes (COP.8), the medication chart (MOM.5), the implant stickers (MOM.7), the consent (PRE.2) and the discharge summary (AAC.7). A hospital that follows these chapters produces files that pass claim scrutiny.

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Scope of services | List of services the hospital provides |
| Triage | Sorting emergency patients by how urgent their condition is |
| Critical value | A test result so abnormal that the doctor must be told at once |
| AERB | Atomic Energy Regulatory Board, licenses X-ray and CT equipment |
| Restraint | Physically or with medicine limiting a patient's movement for safety |
| Antenatal | During pregnancy, before birth |
| Dispensing | The pharmacy issuing medicines against an order |
| Recall | Withdrawing a faulty batch of medicine or device from use |
